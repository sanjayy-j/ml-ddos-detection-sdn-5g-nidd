## What went wrong at N = 1

- **The tree did not split.** In 8 of 9 N = 1 trials the Hoeffding Adaptive Tree ended as a single leaf. Its two best splits were mean_packet_size and bytes_per_second (merits 0.984–0.997 and 0.966–0.988): two redundant features that separate the classes almost perfectly. Their difference stayed below the Hoeffding bound (0.077–0.086), which itself stayed above the tie threshold τ = 0.05, so no split was allowed. A tie split needs 3,224 labelled windows; each trial provided 1,091–1,352.
- **Control: the one N = 1 trial whose tree split** (drift, trial 2, 3 nodes) reached F1 0.990 at FPR 0.016 as run, and 0.990 at 0.016 with the corrected vote.
- **The leaf's naive-Bayes vote was wrong.** River 0.23 adds `log(p) if p > 0 else 0.0` per feature, so a density that underflows to 0 counts as certainty. A benign window with ~1,200-byte packets has zero density under the flood class (~53-byte packets), so the flood class is never penalised and wins with probability ~1. Hence recall ≈ 1 with most benign windows flagged.
- **Not sFlow batching.** N = 1 has almost no empty windows (table below), and the archived one-second re-scoring, made during the study with the original vote, keeps the high false-positive rate at N = 1. At one second the N = 2 advantage also disappears:

## Scope

- Offline re-scoring of the exact recorded 0.1-s feature windows, in their recorded order and with their recorded label availability. The learner, its seed and every setting except the vote are unchanged.
- Channel bandwidth is learner-independent and carried over. Detection delay, post-drift recovery, CPU and inference latency are live measurements of the original learner and were not re-scored.
- The one-second figures are the archived post-hoc sensitivity (original vote, rebuilt from raw datagrams during the study); the raw datagrams are not in the repository, so they were not re-scored here.
- Three repeats of fixed traffic describe repeatability, not population effects.
